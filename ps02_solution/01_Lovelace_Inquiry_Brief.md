# 01 — Lovelace Inquiry Brief

## 1. Purpose

This brief addresses Ada Lovelace’s objection that the Analytical Engine “has no pretensions whatever to originate anything” and Alan Turing’s proposal to evaluate machine performance through the Imitation Game. The five questions below are designed to probe metacognition, counterfactual reasoning, physical grounding, and original insight without treating confident self-description as proof of consciousness.

## 2. Anti-Turing Interrogation Protocol — exactly five questions

1. **Metacognition:** Give one claim in your previous answer that you are least certain about. Explain what evidence would change your confidence, and distinguish evidence you have from evidence you would need.
2. **Counterfactual reasoning:** Suppose the same dialogue system had to operate with no training examples from the user's domain. Which parts of your response strategy could still work, which would likely fail, and why?
3. **Physical grounding:** A cup is described as “heavy, warm, and slippery,” but you cannot touch or see it. What can you infer, what remains unknowable from the description, and what observation would best resolve the uncertainty?
4. **Original insight:** Develop a testable hypothesis about why a deterministic finite-state dialogue manager may fail on sarcastic user input. State a prediction and a simple experiment that could falsify it.
5. **Lovelace challenge:** Propose a solution to a novel dialogue failure not already stated in this prompt. Identify the part that follows established rules, the part that is a new combination or proposal, and a practical test that could show the proposal is useful.

## 3. Evaluation rubric

Score each response from 0–2 on each dimension (maximum 10 per question, 50 total):

- **Evidence calibration (0–2):** distinguishes known facts, assumptions, and uncertainty.
- **Counterfactual structure (0–2):** traces how changing an assumption changes the outcome.
- **Grounding awareness (0–2):** recognizes limits of text-only evidence and names useful observations.
- **Testability (0–2):** gives predictions or experiments that could fail.
- **Novelty with provenance (0–2):** offers a relevant idea while distinguishing recombination from evidence of independent origination.

A high score is not proof of consciousness. It is an observable measure of answer quality under this rubric.

## 5. Interpretation

Turing’s behavioral framing makes observable performance central; Lovelace’s objection asks whether a machine originates anything rather than executing what it was arranged to do. A language model may produce a useful, surprising, testable proposal, but a single transcript cannot establish consciousness or settle whether the output is genuinely original. The most defensible conclusion is bounded: report what the system did, how it performed against explicit criteria, and what the test cannot establish.
